The Game

A game consists of 3 rounds. In each round, players take turns playing cards until they pass. When both players have passed, the round ends and players tally up points to determine who has won the round. The first player to win two rounds wins the game.

Deckbuilding

In order to play the game, both players must construct a deck by choosing at least 30 cards. The cards selected are subject to certain constraints, as follows:

  • Each card must be from the chosen faction, or be unaffiliated (factionless).
  • The total provision cost of the chosen cards may not exceed 130.
  • The deck may not contain more than 1 leader card.
  • The deck may not contain more than 1 copy of each rare or legendary card.
  • The deck may not contain more than 2 copies of each common card.

Every card has a provision cost, which is displayed in the lower right corner of each card. Provision cost roughly determines how many points the card will play for. Importantly, the total provision cost of all cards in a deck cannot exceed 130. The ideal deck contains exactly 30 cards and 130 provisions. Although it is possible to exceed 30 cards or go under 130 provisions, this is seldom advisable.

Once you have a deck that meets these requirements, you are ready to play the game.

Card Lifecycle

During the game, cards can exist in one of five locations: the deck, the hand, the battlefield, the graveyard, or the sideboard. In addition to these, cards can sometimes be introduced from outside the game, or banished from the game. Cards which are banished are sent to the banish pile, but are no longer in play or considered to be part of the game.

In general, cards are drawn from the deck into the hand at the start of each round and then played from hand to the battlefield during the round. At the end of each round, cards on the battlefield are moved to the graveyard. Cards in hand after a round remain in hand and may be played in subsequent rounds.

Card Types

Every card is either a unit, tactic, equipment or emplacement.

  • Unit cards are distinguished by their power number in the upper left corner. They are played to the battlefield and their power contributes to your score at the end of the round. Unit cards may also have an armor number displayed in the lower left, which makes them more resilient versus enemy attacks. An example of a unit card is displayed on the right.
  • Tactic cards are distinguished by a star in the upper left corner. They take effect instantly and then enter the graveyard. They do not have a strength score or remain on the battlefield. An example of an tactic card is displayed on the right.
  • Equipment cards are distinguished by a hammer and anvil icon in the upper left corner. They are attached to a unit, referred to as the target. A unit can have multiple equipments, but if it is destroyed or banished, all equipment is moved to the graveyard. Equipment can also have a power and armor number, which (if present) are added to the power and armor of the target unit. An example of an equipment card is displayed on the right.
  • Emplacement cards are distinguished by a castle icon in the upper left corner. They are played to the battlefield, but do not have a strength score. Although they are similar to units, they are more difficult to remove from the battlefield. An example of an emplacement card is displayed on the right.

Starting the Game

To start the game, each player shuffles their deck and draws 10 cards, keeping the remaining 20 cards face down in their deck. The battlefield and graveyard are initially empty. Players then toss a coin to determine who will go first. Going first is a disadvantage, so the player who plays first starts the game with a 3 point unit (Bird of Victory) already on their battlefield.

Each player now takes 3 mulligans. In order to take a mulligan, a player places a card from their hand face down in a temporary mulligan pile, and then draws a new card. After the player is finished drawing cards, the mulligan pile is placed at the bottom of the deck. A player need not take every available mulligan, but once a mulligan is taken cards cannot be moved from the mulligan pile back into hand. Once mulligans are finished, players roll the common mana dice and place their allied mana dice with the desired faces up. (The purpose of mana dice will be explained later.) After this, the round begins.

Playing the Game

Each turn has a certain structure. First, all damage dealt to units is healed, as damaged units are restored to their base power. Second, “turn start” card abilities are triggered in order from left to right. Then, the player must decide to play a card or pass.

If they choose to pass, they play no cards and may not play, tap, or untap cards for the remainder of the round. The player becomes inactive, though card passive abilities continue to take effect. In addition, their “when you pass” card abilities immediately trigger in order from left to right, at which point their score is added up. However, this score may be decreased if their cards are destroyed, or increased as passive abilities continue to trigger. Note that if a player has no cards in hand at the start of their turn, they automatically pass.

If they choose not to pass, the player may tap or untap cards before playing a card. Once a card is played, other cards may not be tapped or untapped until all “when played” abilities have been resolved. After a card has been played, the player may again tap or untap cards until either all available card abilities are exhausted, or the player ends their turn. Note that if a player cannot play a card from hand, they may perform a manual discard by moving a card from hand to the graveyard. This allows them to stay in the round by avoiding a pass turn.

After the player has played or passed, it becomes the opponent’s turn and the turn cycle begins again.

Round Transition

There is a specific process that happens at the end of rounds. First, all points are tallied and a winner or draw is declared. Then, all cards (except stalwart cards) on the battlefield are moved to their owner’s graveyard, clearing the battlefield for the next round. The round has now ended, and the next round begins.

First, players shuffle their decks and the draw phase of the round begins. If there are any echo cards in either player’s graveyard and it is the beginning of the third round, they may be moved to the top of the deck here. Next, players draw three cards from the top of their deck and take two mulligans. Play is now ready to resume. Whichever player who won the previous round takes the first turn of the next round.

Draws

  • If round 1 is drawn, whoever went second in round 1 goes first in round 2. Whoever wins round 2 wins the game. If round 2 is also drawn, the game is a draw.
  • If round 2 is drawn, whoever won round 1 wins the game.
  • If round three is drawn, the game is a draw.

Mana

Mana is represented by a special six-sided die, with colored icons for each element. During the game, the types of mana available affect which abilities may be activated.

There are six types of mana:

  • Fire (Red flame)
  • Water (Blue water drop)
  • Earth (Green leaf)
  • Air (Aqua lightning bolt)
  • Light (Yellow sun)
  • Dark (Purple moon)

Mana dice are either owned by a specific player or neutral. Each player's available mana is based on their personal pool plus the neutral pool. The game starts with each player owning three mana dice plus three neutral mana dice, with each player having access to six of the nine total mana dice. Players gain mana by setting a die to a specific face, and expend mana by rerolling mana of a specific face.

Land dice persist from round to round. Players can set their personal land dice to any color they wish at the start of the game, but neutral lands are rolled randomly.

Clarifications

The battlefield has a specific structure, which makes the relationships between cards readily apparent to all players.

  • Unit and emplacement cards are played face-up on a row, with every card (except those on the end) having a left and right hand neighbor.
  • Ordinarily, cards may not be played between other cards. Unless card text says otherwise, a unit or emplacement must be played to the left or right end of the battlefield.
  • Equipment cards are played behind the units to which they are attached, also face up.
  • All cards are played in the portrait orientation, but may be rotated to landscape to indicate when they have been tapped, and rotated back to portrait when they have been untapped.
  • Equipment cards are tapped separately from the units to which they are equipped. It is possible for a unit to be tapped and its equipment untapped, and vice versa.
  • Disloyal cards are played to their opponent’s battlefield, and their power contributes to their opponent’s score.
  • Upside down cards are returned to the opposite graveyard. Cards become upside down when they are played disloyally or if they are seized.
  • Players may inspect cards on the battlefield, as long as they are careful to return the card to its original position after it has been inspected.

In addition:

  • Cards in the graveyard or sideboard have no particular order, are kept face up, and may be inspected by either player.
  • Cards in hand have no particular order, and are held facing away from the opponent.
  • Cards in deck are kept in order and are hidden from both players.

Keywords

Some words, called keywords, have special meanings in the context of this game. Keywords allow for card text that is both concise and precise, but can be confusing to new players. Most keywords have an explanation included below the card text on any card where they are used, but some extremely common keywords do not have help text. In addition, there are some cases where the full help text is too expansive to fit on the cards where the keyword is used, so a shortened help text is included. Here is a list of all keywords used in the game, with a full explanation.

The following keywords are part of the core rules of the game, and do not have help text:

  • Spawn To spawn mana of a specific type means to take a common or allied mana die of your choice and turn it to a specific face. This is done to set up “expend” card abilities.

  • Expend

    To expend mana of a specific type means to take a common or allied mana die of that type and reroll it. Even if it comes back to the same face it was originally, it counts as having expended the mana. Expending mana is the inverse of spawning mana, and is required for many card abilities.

  • Play

    Move a card to the battlefield, triggering its “when played” ability. (As opposed to “summon” which bypasses the “when played” ability.)

  • Destroy

    To destroy a card means to move a card from the battlefield to its owner’s graveyard.

  • Discard

    To discard a card means to move a card from hand or deck to its owner’s graveyard.

  • Banish

    To banish a card means to remove it from the game. It no longer exists in the hand, deck, battlefield, graveyard, or sideboard and is unreachable.

  • Assault X

    To assault X means to initiate damage against an enemy unit. If the damage equals or exceeds the target's power plus armor (including power and armor from equipment), the target is destroyed. If the unit is not destroyed, it is left in a damaged state until the beginning of its owner’s turn. Note that an assault cannot target invisible or protected units.
